# ASD-STE100 condensed rule set

Simplified Technical English (STE) is a controlled language for technical documentation. The full specification pairs a restricted dictionary with about 53 writing rules. This file condenses the
writing rules for everyday use in plans, guides, runbooks, and other opinionless documents. It is a summary, not the standard; consult the official ASD-STE100 issue for normative wording.

## Words

- Use a word only with its approved meaning. "Follow" means "come after", not "obey". When the dictionary does not cover a domain term, define it once and use it consistently.
- One word, one meaning. Do not use two words for the same thing ("delete" and "remove"), and do not use one word for two things.
- Prefer the verb form. "Adjust the valve", not "make an adjustment to the valve".
- Keep noun clusters to three nouns maximum. "Valve housing cover" is the limit. Break longer clusters with "of" and "for".
- Use the simplest approved synonym. "Use" over "utilize", "show" over "demonstrate", "start" over "initiate".

## Sentences

- Instructions: 20 words maximum. Descriptions: 25 words maximum.
- One instruction per sentence. If two actions happen at the same time, say so explicitly.
- Active voice. Name the actor: "The pump moves the coolant", not "The coolant is moved".
- Procedures use the imperative: "Close the valve." Descriptions use simple tenses: present for facts, past for completed work, future only for genuine predictions.
- Do not omit articles ("a", "an", "the") or connective words to save space. Telegram style causes misreads.
- Vary nothing for variety's sake. If two sentences describe the same kind of step, give them the same shape.

## Paragraphs and structure

- One topic per paragraph. Six sentences per descriptive paragraph is the working limit.
- Lead with the point. Warnings and cautions come before the step they protect, never after.
- Start warnings with a clear command: "Do not open the panel while the system is energized."
- Lists show parallel items. Numbered lists show ordered steps. Do not mix the two.

## Punctuation

- Periods end sentences. Commas separate clauses and list items. Colons introduce lists. Hyphens join compound modifiers.
- Avoid semicolons, dashes, parentheses, and quotation marks for emphasis. Rewrite the sentence instead.

## Checks before publishing

- Read each sentence as a non-native speaker with no context would. If a word could carry two meanings, swap it.
- Search the document for synonyms of your key terms. Replace every variant with the chosen term.
- Count the words in every instruction. Anything over 20 gets split.
- Find every passive construction. Name the actor or justify the omission.
- Remove every opinion marker: "clearly", "simply", "obviously", "just". Facts need no endorsement.
